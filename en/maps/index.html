Where the geese had it easier

Overall, the geese win 52% of games. Here is where they win more and where less: the dot sits at the geese's win rate, the dashed line marks the overall level. Dot size is the number of games, the same number as in the previous section.

The whiskers show how far the number could wander: if the group played as many games again, the rate would almost certainly land somewhere inside them. Every map's whiskers cross the dashed line: no map clearly differs from the overall level, and the order among them means nothing.

Fun fact. The Carnival collected the most duck wins: 23, more than anywhere else.

How the geese won

The geese can win in two ways, and the end screen tells them apart. Wipe out the ducks: the ducks are found and voted out at meetings, or killed. Finish the tasks: the ducks were never found, but ran out of time before they could close the game out. The end screen names a task victory separately.

The axis at the bottom runs between the two: the further left a dot, the more often games ended with the ducks wiped out; the further right, the more often the geese got their tasks done.

On the right: task wins out of all goose wins on the map. The poles are Eagleton Springs (9 of 13) and S.S. Mother Goose (0 of 19), and their whiskers don't overlap: the gap is real, not chance.

Fun fact. S.S. Mother Goose: 19 wins for the geese and not one by tasks. Either the ducks were always found there, or nobody ever got round to the tasks.

Who survived to the end

Vertically: the share of players still alive on the end screen; horizontally: how many people were in the lobby. Dot size is the number of games. Maps with fewer than ten games are left out: one or two games would stretch the scale and squash the rest. Food for thought, not a conclusion.

Neutrals: it's about the roles, not the maps

Splitting the neutrals by map as well doesn't work: even the Dodo Bird, dealt 301 times across all evenings, spreads over 10 maps, which leaves single digits of wins per cell, and any rate there is noise. So there is one axis for all of them here: how many times the role was dealt and how many times it won.

Same threshold: for roles dealt fewer than ten times the rate means nothing, and they are shown faded.

Fun fact. Since the Magpie arrived, from evening #99, roughly one game in 5 had no killing neutral at all: the three neutral seats went to the Magpie, Pigeon and Dodo Bird. That is 30 of 138 games (21.7%), or 31.9% of all games with the Magpie. The ducks won 33% of those games (10 of 30), against 19% in the rest (21 of 108). Ducks seem to have it easier, but there are few games yet: the whiskers of the two rates overlap, and the gap may be chance.
